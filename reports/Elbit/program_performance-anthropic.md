### PROGRAM 1: F-35 Gen III Helmet-Mounted Display System (HMDS)

**Executing Entity:** Collins Elbit Vision Systems (CEVS) – a joint venture between Collins Aerospace and Elbit Systems of America

**Contract Status:** Active Production - Full-Rate Production

**Delivery Performance:**
CEVS announced that it has delivered the 3,000th F-35 Gen III Helmet Mounted Display Systems (HMDS) to the Joint Strike Fighter as of February 2024. This represents a significant production milestone.

**Technical Performance:**
- The F-35 Gen III HMDS is the world's most advanced helmet-mounted display system. Its next-generation user interface serves as a pilot's primary display system, providing them with intuitive access to vital flight, tactical and sensor information day or night.
- "Zero-G – the newest generation of our HMDS – is the lightest, most capable and safest helmet mounted display system ever developed," said Elbit America's Jeff Hoberg. "It was designed for next-generation fighter aircraft platforms and can also support 4th and 5th generation aircraft."

**Historical Technical Issues (RESOLVED):**
One of the F-35 Lightning II's most impressive and controversial components is the $400,000 helmet produced by a joint venture between Rockwell Collins and Elbit Systems of America. Plagued by issues — and criticism — throughout its development, the Gen III Helmet-Mounted Display System (HMDS) is finally in use and the companies behind it say pilots are effusive about its performance.

There were a handful of specific issues that Rockwell, Elbit and Lockheed had to work out before the helmet would be ready for operational use. One involved a blooming effect during night-time usage that was especially problematic for F-35Cs trying to land on aircraft carriers.

A BAE Systems helmet was considered when HMDS development was experiencing significant problems, but these issues were eventually worked out. The Helmet-Mounted Display System was fully operational and ready for delivery in July 2014.

**Customer Satisfaction Indicators:**
- Continuous production deliveries over multiple years
- 3,000+ units delivered indicates stable production
- Next-generation Zero-G system development indicates continued customer interest

**Risk Assessment:** LOW - Program in mature FRP phase with resolved historical issues

### PROGRAM 3: ENVG-B (Enhanced Night Vision Goggle - Binocular)

**Contract Structure:**
L3Harris and Elbit Systems of America have each received production contracts worth potentially $442 million from the Army to deliver the next-generation Enhanced Night Vision Goggle-Binocular (ENVG-B) devices.

**Recent Orders:**
- October 2022: Elbit Systems of America was awarded an order valued at approximately $107 million for the supply of Enhanced Night Vision Goggle – Binocular (ENVG-B) systems, spare parts, logistics support, and test equipment, for the U.S. Army. The order will be executed in Roanoke, Virginia and will be supplied during the years 2023 and 2024.

- January 2025: Elbit Systems of America has secured a delivery order exceeding $139 million for the production of Enhanced Night Vision Goggle – Binocular (ENVG-B) systems for the U.S. Army. The order, placed under an Indefinite Delivery/Indefinite Quantity (ID/IQ) contract awarded in 2023, includes spare parts and logistical support, with work to be conducted at the company's facility in Roanoke, Virginia. Deliveries under this contract are scheduled to run through December 2026.

**Delivery Performance:**
The U.S. Army has awarded Elbit Systems of America a $139 million delivery order to continue producing Enhanced Night Vision Goggle-Binocular, or ENVG-B, systems for the military branch. Since 2020, the company has delivered more than 25,000 units of night vision binocular systems to service personnel through the Army's ENVG-B program and Squad Binocular Night Vision Goggle program of the U.S. Marine Corps.

**Technical Specifications:**
ENVG-Bs are equipped with high performance white phosphor image intensifier tubes for better scene contrast providing soldiers better situational awareness in the dark, but also when there is fog, dust, or smoke. The ENVG-B system includes a wireless connection to soldiers' rifle-mounted thermal weapon site and augmented reality overlay, enabling rapid target acquisition and improved situational awareness in various battlefield conditions.

## PART 3: TECHNICAL PERFORMANCE AND TESTING

### F-35 HMDS - Historical Issues (Tier 1 Source: Historical trade reporting)

The F-35 HMDS program experienced well-documented technical challenges during development:

1. **Night Vision Blooming:** "One involved a blooming effect during night-time usage that was especially problematic for F-35Cs trying to land on aircraft carriers."

2. **Backlight Bleed-Through:** "The thing that they discovered with our previous display technology, which was active-matrix LCD...was that there was a certain amount of bleed-through of our back-lighting technology...So they would sort of see this green haze in front of them from the backlight, and it was making it difficult for them to find the aircraft carrier."

3. **Resolution Status:** Issues were resolved in Gen III production, now meeting specifications

### Current Technical Status

The state-of-the-art HMD features a biocular 40×30 degrees field-of-view, high brightness and high resolution display, with integrated digital night vision. When fully integrated with the aircraft sensors and systems, the HMDS provides the F-35 pilot with unparalleled situational awareness.

## PART 7: RED 6 COMPETITIVE IMPLICATIONS

### OPPORTUNITIES FOR RED 6 POSITIONING

**1. Historical F-35 HMDS Development Challenges**
- While resolved, the documented issues with night vision, weight, and display quality during development demonstrate the technical complexity of advanced HMD systems
- Red 6 opportunity: Position AR solution as purpose-built for training (less demanding than combat) with faster development timeline

**2. U.S. Air Force F-16 JHMCS Replacement**
- The U.S. Air Force and Air National Guard replaced JHMCS with Scorpion HMIT on F-16C
- This demonstrates Elbit does NOT have invincible incumbent position
- Red 6 opportunity: USAF has shown willingness to change HMD vendors

**3. Training Simulation Integration Gaps**
- Elbit's simulation systems (MTC, OneSim) are platform-specific
- Red 6 opportunity: Offer aircraft-agnostic AR training capability

### BARRIERS TO DISPLACEMENT

**1. Installed Base Advantage**
- For over 23 years, we have had the leading combat-proven helmet-mounted display for fixed-wing aircraft, including F/A-18, F-15, and the F-16.
- Our HMD systems are a daily companion for thousands of fighter pilots worldwide.
- Integration with existing aircraft systems creates switching costs

**2. Joint Venture Structure**
- CEVS partnership with Collins Aerospace (RTX) provides access to major prime contractor relationships
- Adds credibility and reduces customer procurement risk

**3. Long-Term Contract Structures**
- 15-18 year contracts with Israel include O&M
- Creates long-term revenue lock-in

**4. Production Scale**
- 3,000+ F-35 helmets, 25,000+ night vision units demonstrates manufacturing capability at scale
- Red 6 would need to demonstrate comparable production readiness

### LESSONS LEARNED FOR RED 6

**From Elbit's Success:**
1. Customer-centric development: "The team followed a new development process that incorporated early and continuous pilot input"
2. Joint venture strategy provides market access and risk sharing
3. Sustainment contracts provide revenue stability
4. International market diversification reduces single-customer risk

**From Elbit's Challenges:**
1. Night vision/low-light performance is critical - test extensively before fielding
2. Weight and comfort matter for pilot acceptance
3. Integration with aircraft systems is complex and time-consuming
